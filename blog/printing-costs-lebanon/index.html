Understanding Printing Costs in Lebanon: A Complete Guide for 2026

July 28, 2026Samer Jawhar

If you've ever requested a printing quote, you know the prices can vary dramatically from one printer to another — and even from one job to the next at the same printer. This isn't arbitrary. Printing is a complex manufacturing process with many variables, and understanding them will help you make better decisions and get the best value for your project.

What Goes Into a Printing Price?

Every printed product is the sum of several cost components. Here's the breakdown:

  • Paper / Substrate — The single biggest material cost. Coated, uncoated, recycled, specialty, or board — each has a different price point. Bulk buying power matters a lot here.
  • Prepress & Plates — File preparation, proofing, and plate-making (for offset). Digital printing skips plates but still has setup time.
  • Printing (Machine Time) — Offset presses cost more to set up but are cheaper per unit at volume. Digital has no setup but higher per-click costs.
  • Finishing — Cutting, folding, binding, laminating, foiling, embossing, UV. Each finish is a separate pass with its own setup and run cost.
  • Markup & Margin — Printers apply a markup to cover overhead, expertise, and profit. This varies widely.

Key insight: The markup on a job is not pure profit — it covers the printer's expertise, equipment maintenance, quality control, and the risk of anything going wrong. At Aleph, we apply a consistent 120% markup across all jobs, which is conservative by industry standards.

Quantity: The Biggest Lever

The most important factor in unit price is quantity. Fixed costs (prepress, plates, machine setup) are spread across the run. A business card that costs $0.50 each at 100 units drops to $0.08 at 1,000 and $0.03 at 5,000.

This is why printers ask "how many?" before anything else. It determines which production method makes sense and how the price scales.

Paper Grades & Selection

Lebanon's printing market has access to a wide range of paper stocks. Common choices include:

  • Art paper (coated) — Smooth, glossy or matte. Ideal for brochures and magazines.
  • Artboard (carton) — Thick, rigid. Used for packaging and business cards.
  • Offset (uncoated) — Natural finish, easier to write on. Good for stationery and books.
  • Specialty — Cotton (Fabriano, Conqueror), metallized, security substrates, textured.

Choosing the right paper is a balance between aesthetics, durability, and budget. A good printer will recommend options you may not have considered.

Finishing Adds Up

Finishing is often where the biggest surprises appear in a quote. Lamination, foiling, die-cutting, and hand-assembly each involve labour and machine time. A rigid box with foil stamping, embossing, and a ribbon may have more cost in finishing than in printing.

When budgeting, ask your printer which finishes add the most visual impact for the cost. Often a single well-placed foil detail is more effective than multiple passes of different finishes.

Offset vs. Digital at Different Quantities

As a rule of thumb:

  • Under 500 units — Digital is almost always more economical.
  • 500–2,000 units — It depends on the complexity. Digital offers flexibility; offset offers lower per-unit cost.
  • Over 2,000 units — Offset is typically the better value, especially for full-colour work.

Aleph operates both Heidelberg offset presses and digital equipment, so we can recommend the right method for your specific run.

How to Get the Best Printing Value

  • Prepare your files properly (bleeds, margins, colour mode) to avoid prepress charges.
  • Combine multiple items into one print run when possible.
  • Ask about paper alternatives — a small change can significantly lower cost.
  • Be clear about your deadline. Rush charges are real.
  • Build a relationship with your printer. Consistent clients get better service and pricing over time.
